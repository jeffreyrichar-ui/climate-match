## Ethical Theory

_18 schools_

- **Act Utilitarianism** — Consequentialism ▸ Utilitarianism ▸ Act Utilitarianism
  Each individual act is judged right if and only if it produces the greatest overall good compared to available alternatives.
  <sub>Key thinkers: J.J.C. Smart, Peter Singer · 20th c. · Britain, Australia</sub>
- **Care Ethics** _(also: Ethics of Care)_ — Feminist Ethics ▸ Care Ethics
  Morality is grounded in responsiveness, attentiveness, and responsibility within concrete caring relationships rather than abstract impartial principles.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Carol Gilligan, Nel Noddings, Virginia Held · 20th c. · United States</sub>
- **Classical Utilitarianism** _(also: Hedonistic Utilitarianism)_ — Consequentialism ▸ Utilitarianism ▸ Classical Utilitarianism
  The morally right action is the one that maximizes aggregate pleasure and minimizes pain across all affected sentient beings.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Jeremy Bentham, John Stuart Mill · 18th-19th c. · Britain</sub>
- **Consequentialism** _(also: Teleological Ethics)_ — Consequentialism
  The moral status of an action depends solely on the goodness of its outcomes, independent of intentions or rule-conformity.
  <sub>Key thinkers: G.E. Moore, Philip Pettit, Derek Parfit · 20th c. · Britain</sub>
- **Contractualism** _(also: Scanlonian Contractualism)_ — Social Contract Ethics ▸ Contractualism
  An act is wrong if it violates principles that no one could reasonably reject as a basis for informed, unforced general agreement.
  <sub>Key thinkers: T.M. Scanlon · 20th-21st c. · United States</sub>
- **Ethical Altruism** _(also: Effective Altruism (ethical core))_ — Consequentialism ▸ Ethical Altruism
  Agents have a strong moral obligation to act for the benefit of others, often impartially maximizing the good done for all.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Auguste Comte, Peter Singer, Peter Unger · 19th-21st c. · Europe, United States</sub>
- **Ethical Egoism** — Egoism ▸ Ethical Egoism
  Each person morally ought to act in whatever way maximally promotes their own long-term self-interest.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Ayn Rand, Max Stirner, Henry Sidgwick (analyst) · 19th-20th c. · Europe, United States</sub>
- **Ethical Intuitionism** _(also: Moral Intuitionism)_ — Metaethics ▸ Ethical Intuitionism
  Basic moral truths are known non-inferentially through rational intuition, and irreducible moral properties are real and self-evident.
  <sub>Key thinkers: G.E. Moore, H.A. Prichard, Michael Huemer · 20th-21st c. · Britain, United States</sub>
- **Gauthierian Contractarianism** _(also: Morals by Agreement)_ — Social Contract Ethics ▸ Contractarianism ▸ Gauthierian Contractarianism
  Morality is a system of rational constraints on self-interest that maximizing agents would accept as mutually beneficial bargains.
  <sub>Key thinkers: David Gauthier · 20th c. · Canada, United States</sub>
- **Hobbesian Contractarianism** _(also: Classical Social Contract Theory)_ — Social Contract Ethics ▸ Contractarianism ▸ Hobbesian Contractarianism
  Moral and political norms arise as mutually advantageous agreements among self-interested agents seeking to escape a violent state of nature.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Thomas Hobbes · 17th c. · England</sub>
- **Kantian Deontology** _(also: Kantian Ethics)_ — Deontology ▸ Kantian Deontology
  Morality consists in acting from duty according to universalizable maxims that treat rational agents always as ends, never merely as means.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Immanuel Kant, Christine Korsgaard, Onora O'Neill · 18th c. · Prussia, Germany</sub>
- **Modern Virtue Ethics** _(also: Neo-Aristotelian Virtue Ethics)_ — Virtue Ethics ▸ Modern Virtue Ethics
  Ethics centers on cultivating stable character traits and practical wisdom that constitute human flourishing rather than following rules or maximizing outcomes.
  <sub>Key thinkers: G.E.M. Anscombe, Alasdair MacIntyre, Philippa Foot, Rosalind Hursthouse · 20th c. · Britain</sub>
- **Moral Error Theory** _(also: Moral Nihilism, Moral Skepticism)_ — Metaethics ▸ Moral Error Theory
  Moral judgments purport to state objective facts but all such claims are systematically false because no objective moral properties exist.
  <sub>Key thinkers: J.L. Mackie, Richard Joyce · 20th c. · Australia, Britain</sub>
- **Moral Relativism** _(also: Ethical Relativism)_ — Metaethics ▸ Moral Relativism
  Moral truths are not universal but hold only relative to the norms of particular cultures, communities, or individual frameworks.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Gilbert Harman, David Wong, Ruth Benedict · 20th c. · United States</sub>
- **Negative Utilitarianism** — Consequentialism ▸ Utilitarianism ▸ Negative Utilitarianism
  The primary moral duty is to minimize suffering, giving reduction of harm priority over the promotion of positive happiness.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Karl Popper (attributed), Hermann Vetter · 20th c. · Britain, Europe</sub>
- **Preference Utilitarianism** — Consequentialism ▸ Utilitarianism ▸ Preference Utilitarianism
  Rightness maximizes the satisfaction of the informed preferences of all affected parties rather than pleasure or happiness as such.
  <sub>Key thinkers: R.M. Hare, Peter Singer (early) · 20th c. · Britain, Australia</sub>
- **Rossian Pluralist Deontology** _(also: Ethical Intuitionism of Prima Facie Duties)_ — Deontology ▸ Rossian Pluralist Deontology
  Morality comprises multiple self-evident prima facie duties whose conflicts are resolved by judgment about which duty is most stringent.
  <sub>Key thinkers: W.D. Ross · 20th c. · Britain</sub>
- **Rule Utilitarianism** — Consequentialism ▸ Utilitarianism ▸ Rule Utilitarianism
  An action is right if it conforms to a rule whose general adoption would maximize overall welfare more than alternative rules.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Richard Brandt, John Harsanyi · 20th c. · United States</sub>

## Modern Secular

_17 schools_

- **Antitheism** _(also: Militant Atheism)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Nonbelief ▸ Antitheism
  An active opposition to theism and religion, holding belief in gods to be false and typically harmful to human flourishing.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Christopher Hitchens, Richard Dawkins, Sam Harris · 20th-21st c. · Anglophone West</sub>
- **Atheism** _(also: Nontheism)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Nonbelief ▸ Atheism
  The absence or rejection of belief in the existence of any deity, ranging from a lack of belief to positive denial.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Baron d'Holbach, Ludwig Feuerbach, Bertrand Russell · 18th-20th c. · Europe</sub>
- **British Empiricism** _(also: Classical Empiricism)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Empiricism ▸ British Empiricism
  The tradition holding that all substantive knowledge derives from sense experience rather than innate ideas or pure reason.
  <sub>Key thinkers: John Locke, David Hume · 17th-18th c. · Britain</sub>
- **Continental Rationalism** _(also: Cartesian Rationalism)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Rationalism ▸ Continental Rationalism
  The tradition holding that reason and innate ideas, rather than sense experience, are the primary source of certain knowledge.
  <sub>Key thinkers: René Descartes, G.W. Leibniz · 17th c. · Continental Europe</sub>
- **Deweyan Instrumentalism** _(also: Experimentalism)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Pragmatism ▸ Instrumentalism
  Dewey's view that ideas and theories are instruments for solving problems and guiding action, tested by their practical outcomes.
  <sub>Key thinkers: John Dewey · 20th c. · United States</sub>
- **Freethought** _(also: Freethinking)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Nonbelief ▸ Freethought
  A stance holding that beliefs should be formed by logic, reason, and evidence rather than tradition, authority, or dogma.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Robert G. Ingersoll, Charles Bradlaugh, Anthony Collins · 17th-19th c. · Britain & United States</sub>
- **Logical Positivism** _(also: Logical Empiricism, Vienna Circle)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Positivism ▸ Logical Positivism
  The movement holding that only empirically verifiable or analytically true statements are cognitively meaningful, dismissing metaphysics as nonsense.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Moritz Schlick, Rudolf Carnap, A.J. Ayer, Otto Neurath · 1920s-1930s · Vienna & Berlin</sub>
- **Peircean Pragmaticism** _(also: Pragmaticism)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Pragmatism ▸ Pragmaticism
  Peirce's rigorous variant tying a concept's meaning to its conceivable experimental effects, with truth as the endpoint of inquiry.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Charles Sanders Peirce · late 19th-early 20th c. · United States</sub>
- **Philosophical Naturalism** _(also: Metaphysical Naturalism)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Naturalism ▸ Philosophical Naturalism
  The view that reality consists solely of the natural world governed by natural laws, admitting no supernatural entities or causes.
  <sub>Key thinkers: W.V.O. Quine, John Dewey, Ernest Nagel · 20th c. · North America</sub>
- **Physicalism** _(also: Materialism)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Naturalism ▸ Physicalism
  The thesis that everything that exists is physical, or supervenes on the physical, including mind and consciousness.
  <sub>Key thinkers: J.J.C. Smart, David Armstrong, Paul & Patricia Churchland · 20th c. · Australia & Anglophone West</sub>
- **Positivism** _(also: Comtean Positivism)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Positivism ▸ Comtean Positivism
  Comte's doctrine that genuine knowledge derives only from empirical science, with societies progressing through theological, metaphysical, and positive stages.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Auguste Comte, Émile Littré · 19th c. · France</sub>
- **Pragmatism** _(also: Classical Pragmatism)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Pragmatism ▸ Classical Pragmatism
  The tradition holding that the meaning and truth of ideas lie in their practical consequences and success in experience.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Charles Sanders Peirce, William James, John Dewey · late 19th-20th c. · United States</sub>
- **Scientific Instrumentalism** _(also: Anti-realism about theories)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Philosophy of Science ▸ Instrumentalism
  The philosophy of science position that scientific theories are useful predictive tools rather than literally true descriptions of unobservable reality.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Ernst Mach, Pierre Duhem, Bas van Fraassen · 19th-20th c. · Europe</sub>
- **Scientism** _(also: Scientific Imperialism)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Naturalism ▸ Scientism
  The view that the methods of natural science are the only or best route to genuine knowledge across all domains of inquiry.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Alex Rosenberg, associated with logical empiricist heirs · 20th-21st c. · Anglophone West</sub>
- **Secular Buddhism** _(also: Naturalized Buddhism)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Naturalized Contemplative ▸ Secular Buddhism
  A naturalistic reinterpretation of Buddhist practice and ethics that sets aside supernatural rebirth and karma in favor of this-life flourishing.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Stephen Batchelor, Owen Flanagan · late 20th-21st c. · Western & global</sub>
- **Secular Humanism** _(also: Ethical Humanism)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Humanism ▸ Secular Humanism
  A worldview grounding ethics and meaning in human reason, empathy, and dignity without appeal to the supernatural or divine authority.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Paul Kurtz, Corliss Lamont, A.C. Grayling · 20th c. · Europe & North America</sub>
- **Spinozism** _(also: God-or-Nature Monism)_ — Modern Secular ▸ Rationalism ▸ Spinozism
  Spinoza's system identifying God with Nature as a single infinite substance, yielding a deterministic universe knowable through reason.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Baruch Spinoza · 17th c. · Dutch Republic</sub>

## Political & Social

_17 schools_

- **Anarcho-Syndicalism** — Anarchism ▸ Anarcho-Syndicalism
  A revolutionary current holding that self-managed labor unions can overthrow capitalism through direct action and general strike, then run society.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Rudolf Rocker, Fernand Pelloutier, Georges Sorel · Late 19th–20th c. · France / Spain</sub>
- **Burkean Conservatism** _(also: Traditionalist Conservatism)_ — Conservatism ▸ Burkean Conservatism
  A secular philosophy valuing tradition, gradual reform, inherited institutions, and prudence, distrusting abstract rationalist schemes of radical reconstruction.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Edmund Burke, Michael Oakeshott · 18th–20th c. · Britain</sub>
- **Civic Republicanism** _(also: Neo-Republicanism)_ — Republicanism ▸ Civic Republicanism
  A tradition defining freedom as non-domination, prizing civic virtue, active participation, and institutions that check arbitrary power over citizens.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Niccolò Machiavelli, Philip Pettit, Quentin Skinner · 16th c.; revived 20th c. · Italy / Britain / Australia</sub>
- **Classical Liberalism** — Liberalism ▸ Classical Liberalism
  A philosophy defending individual liberty, private property, rule of law, constitutional government, and free markets against arbitrary state power.
  <sub>Key thinkers: John Locke, Adam Smith, John Stuart Mill · 17th–19th c. · Britain / Western Europe</sub>
- **Classical Marxism** _(also: Historical Materialism)_ — Socialism ▸ Marxism ▸ Classical Marxism
  A materialist theory holding that class struggle over the means of production drives history toward the abolition of capitalism and stateless communism.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Karl Marx, Friedrich Engels · 19th c. · Germany / Western Europe</sub>
- **Communitarianism** — Communitarianism
  A philosophy holding that identity and morality are constituted by community and shared traditions, criticizing liberalism's abstract, unencumbered individual.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Michael Sandel, Charles Taylor, Alasdair MacIntyre, Michael Walzer · Late 20th c. · United States / Canada</sub>
- **Democratic Socialism** _(also: Social Democracy)_ — Socialism ▸ Democratic Socialism
  A philosophy seeking social ownership or regulation of the economy and egalitarian welfare through democratic, non-revolutionary political means.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Eduard Bernstein, Karl Kautsky, Anthony Crosland · Late 19th–20th c. · Western Europe</sub>
- **Dialectical Materialism** _(also: Diamat)_ — Socialism ▸ Marxism ▸ Dialectical Materialism
  A materialist philosophy of nature and history holding that reality develops through contradiction and dialectical laws governing matter in motion.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Friedrich Engels, Georgi Plekhanov, Vladimir Lenin · Late 19th–20th c. · Germany / Russia</sub>
- **Egalitarianism** _(also: Luck Egalitarianism)_ — Egalitarianism
  A family of theories holding that persons are of equal moral worth and that justice requires reducing morally arbitrary inequalities of condition.
  <sub>Key thinkers: R.H. Tawney, Ronald Dworkin, G.A. Cohen, Elizabeth Anderson · 20th c. · Britain / United States</sub>
- **Individualist Anarchism** _(also: Egoist Anarchism)_ — Anarchism ▸ Individualist Anarchism
  A tradition prioritizing the sovereign individual and conscience against the state, sometimes grounded in egoism and voluntary association.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Max Stirner, Josiah Warren, Henry David Thoreau · 19th c. · Germany / United States</sub>
- **Libertarianism** _(also: Right-Libertarianism)_ — Liberalism ▸ Libertarianism
  A philosophy grounding politics in self-ownership and non-aggression, favoring a minimal state, strong property rights, and unregulated markets.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Robert Nozick, Murray Rothbard, Ludwig von Mises · 20th c. · United States</sub>
- **Marxist-Leninism** _(also: Leninism)_ — Socialism ▸ Marxism ▸ Marxist-Leninism
  A revolutionary doctrine holding that a vanguard party leads the proletariat to seize state power and build socialism through a workers' dictatorship.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Vladimir Lenin, Joseph Stalin · 20th c. · Russia / USSR</sub>
- **Mutualism** — Anarchism ▸ Mutualism
  An anarchist economics advocating worker-owned cooperatives, free credit, and market exchange based on labor value without profit or usury.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Pierre-Joseph Proudhon, Benjamin Tucker · 19th c. · France / United States</sub>
- **Rawlsian Justice as Fairness** _(also: Social-Liberal Justice, Egalitarian Liberalism)_ — Liberalism ▸ Rawlsian Social-Liberalism
  A social-contract theory deriving principles of justice from an impartial original position, guaranteeing equal liberties and benefiting the least advantaged.
  <sub>Key thinkers: John Rawls · 20th c. · United States</sub>
- **Social Anarchism** _(also: Collectivist Anarchism, Anarcho-Communism)_ — Anarchism ▸ Social Anarchism
  A stateless philosophy abolishing hierarchy and private property in favor of voluntary cooperation, mutual aid, and communal ownership.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Mikhail Bakunin, Peter Kropotkin · 19th c. · Russia / Western Europe</sub>
- **Utopian Socialism** — Socialism ▸ Utopian Socialism
  Early socialist thought proposing ideal cooperative communities and rational social reorganization to abolish poverty, appealing to reason and moral persuasion.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Robert Owen, Charles Fourier, Henri de Saint-Simon · Early 19th c. · Britain / France</sub>
- **Western Marxism** _(also: Critical Marxism, Hegelian Marxism)_ — Socialism ▸ Marxism ▸ Western Marxism
  A humanist current stressing consciousness, culture, and alienation over economic determinism, recovering Hegelian dialectics and the young Marx.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Georg Lukács, Antonio Gramsci, Karl Korsch · 20th c. · Central / Southern Europe</sub>

## Contemporary & Applied

_16 schools_

- **Absurdist Optimism** _(also: Camusian Revolt, Affirmative Absurdism)_ — Contemporary ▸ Existential Lineage ▸ Absurdist Optimism
  A stance holding that though life lacks inherent meaning, one can revolt against the absurd and affirm existence with defiant joy.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Albert Camus, and later affirmative readers · Mid-20th c. CE · France / global</sub>
- **Antinatalism** _(also: Anti-natalism)_ — Contemporary ▸ Applied Ethics ▸ Antinatalism
  The ethical position that procreation is morally wrong or unjustifiable, often because coming into existence entails unavoidable harm.
  <sub>Key thinkers: David Benatar, Peter Wessel Zapffe, Julio Cabrera · 20th–21st c. CE · South Africa / Norway / global</sub>
- **Cosmism** _(also: Russian Cosmism)_ — Contemporary ▸ Philosophy of Technology ▸ Cosmism
  A movement envisioning humanity's expansion into the cosmos, radical life extension, and eventual scientific resurrection and transformation of the species.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Nikolai Fyodorov, Konstantin Tsiolkovsky, Vladimir Vernadsky, Ben Goertzel · Late 19th–20th c. CE · Russia</sub>
- **Deep Ecology** _(also: Ecosophy)_ — Contemporary ▸ Environmental Philosophy ▸ Deep Ecology
  An ecological philosophy asserting the intrinsic worth of all living beings independent of human utility, calling for reduced human impact on nature.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Arne Naess, George Sessions, Bill Devall · 1970s CE · Norway / US</sub>
- **Digital Minimalism** _(also: Attention Minimalism, Attention Ecology)_ — Contemporary ▸ Life-Philosophy ▸ Minimalism ▸ Digital Minimalism
  A philosophy of technology use advocating intentional, selective engagement with digital tools to reclaim attention and support deep, meaningful living.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Cal Newport, Jenny Odell, Tristan Harris · 2010s–2020s CE · US / global</sub>
- **Effective Altruism** _(also: EA)_ — Contemporary ▸ Applied Ethics ▸ Effective Altruism
  A movement using evidence and reason to identify the most effective ways to benefit others and acting on those findings.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Peter Singer, William MacAskill, Toby Ord, Holden Karnofsky · 2000s CE · UK / US (Oxford, Bay Area)</sub>
- **Extropianism** _(also: Extropy, Extropic Thought)_ — Contemporary ▸ Philosophy of Technology ▸ Transhumanism ▸ Extropianism
  An optimistic libertarian strand of transhumanism promoting perpetual progress, self-transformation, and boundless expansion via reason and technology.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Max More, Tom Morrow (Natasha Vita-More) · 1990s CE · US (California)</sub>
- **Gaian Eco-Philosophy** _(also: Gaian Naturalism, Secular Gaianism)_ — Contemporary ▸ Environmental Philosophy ▸ Gaian Eco-Philosophy
  A non-theistic worldview treating the biosphere as a self-regulating system, grounding ethics and meaning in Earth's living planetary whole.
  <sub>Key thinkers: James Lovelock, Lynn Margulis, Stephan Harding · 1970s–2000s CE · UK / US</sub>
- **Longtermism** _(also: Strong Longtermism)_ — Contemporary ▸ Applied Ethics ▸ Longtermism
  The ethical view that positively influencing the long-term future is a key moral priority given the vast number of potential future people.
  <sub>Key thinkers: William MacAskill, Toby Ord, Nick Bostrom, Hilary Greaves · 2010s CE · UK / US</sub>
- **Modern Stoicism** _(also: Stoicism 2.0, Contemporary Stoicism)_ — Contemporary ▸ Revived Hellenistic ▸ Modern Stoicism
  A contemporary revival adapting classical Stoic practices of virtue, emotional regulation, and acceptance for secular self-improvement and psychological resilience.
  <sub>Key thinkers: William Irvine, Massimo Pigliucci, Donald Robertson, Ryan Holiday · 2010s CE · US / UK / global</sub>
- **Philosophical Minimalism** _(also: Simple Living, Voluntary Simplicity)_ — Contemporary ▸ Life-Philosophy ▸ Minimalism
  A life-philosophy that seeks well-being and freedom by deliberately reducing possessions, commitments, and consumption to focus on what one values.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Joshua Fields Millburn, Ryan Nicodemus, Fumio Sasaki, Duane Elgin · 2010s CE · US / Japan / global</sub>
- **Posthumanism** _(also: Critical Posthumanism)_ — Contemporary ▸ Philosophy of Technology ▸ Posthumanism
  A theoretical stance decentering the human, questioning humanist assumptions and reconceiving humans as entangled with technology, animals, and environment.
  <sub>Key thinkers: N. Katherine Hayles, Rosi Braidotti, Donna Haraway, Cary Wolfe · Late 20th–21st c. CE · US / Europe</sub>
- **Rationalist Movement (LessWrong)** _(also: Aspiring Rationalism, LessWrong Rationality)_ — Contemporary ▸ Applied Epistemology ▸ Rationalist Movement
  A community-based project to improve human reasoning through Bayesian epistemology, cognitive-bias awareness, and the systematic pursuit of accurate beliefs.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Eliezer Yudkowsky, Scott Alexander, Julia Galef, Robin Hanson · 2000s–2010s CE · US (Bay Area) / online</sub>
- **Secular Buddhism** _(also: Naturalized Buddhism, Buddhist Modernism)_ — Contemporary ▸ Revived Contemplative ▸ Secular Buddhism
  A naturalistic reinterpretation of Buddhist practice retaining mindfulness and ethics while setting aside supernatural cosmology and metaphysical rebirth.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Stephen Batchelor, Sam Harris, Robert Wright · Late 20th–21st c. CE · US / UK / global</sub>
- **Secular Humanism** _(also: Ethical Humanism)_ — Contemporary ▸ Life-Philosophy ▸ Secular Humanism
  A life-stance grounding ethics, meaning, and human flourishing in reason, science, and shared human values without reference to the supernatural.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Paul Kurtz, Corliss Lamont, A.C. Grayling · 20th–21st c. CE · US / Europe / global</sub>
- **Transhumanism** _(also: H+)_ — Contemporary ▸ Philosophy of Technology ▸ Transhumanism
  A movement advocating the use of science and technology to enhance human intellect, physiology, and lifespan beyond current biological limits.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Max More, Nick Bostrom, FM-2030, Julian Huxley, Ray Kurzweil · Late 20th c. CE · US / Europe</sub>

## Hellenistic

_16 schools_

- **Academic Skepticism** _(also: New Academy)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Skepticism ▸ Academic Skepticism
  Skeptical phase of Plato's Academy denying certain knowledge and arguing for suspension of assent while acting on the merely persuasive or probable.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Arcesilaus, Carneades, Cicero · 3rd–2nd c. BCE · Athens</sub>
- **Cynicism** _(also: The Cynics)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Cynicism
  Ascetic school teaching that virtue is achieved by living self-sufficiently in accord with nature and rejecting wealth, convention, and social pretense.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Antisthenes, Diogenes of Sinope, Crates of Thebes · 4th c. BCE · Ancient Greece</sub>
- **Cyrenaic Hedonism** _(also: Cyrenaicism)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Cyrenaic Hedonism
  Hedonist school holding that immediate bodily pleasure of the present moment is the highest good, with knowledge limited to one's own sensations.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Aristippus of Cyrene, Arete, Theodorus, Hegesias · 4th c. BCE · Cyrene, North Africa</sub>
- **Dialectical School** _(also: The Dialecticians)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Megarian School ▸ Dialectical School
  Offshoot of the Megarians devoted to formal logic and the analysis of modality and conditionals, influencing Stoic propositional logic.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Diodorus Cronus, Philo the Dialectician · 4th–3rd c. BCE · Ancient Greece</sub>
- **Early Stoicism** _(also: Old Stoa)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Stoicism ▸ Early Stoicism
  Founding Stoic school holding that virtue lived in accord with rational cosmic nature is the sole good and sufficient for happiness.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Zeno of Citium, Cleanthes, Chrysippus · 3rd c. BCE · Ancient Greece</sub>
- **Eclecticism** _(also: Philosophical Eclecticism)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Eclecticism
  Method of selecting compatible doctrines from multiple schools rather than adhering to one system, prizing practical reason and common sense.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Cicero, Antiochus of Ascalon, Potamon of Alexandria · 1st c. BCE · Roman world</sub>
- **Epicureanism** _(also: The Garden)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Epicureanism
  Atomist materialist school holding that a tranquil life of modest pleasure, friendship, and freedom from fear of gods and death is the highest good.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Epicurus, Metrodorus, Lucretius · 4th c. BCE · Ancient Greece</sub>
- **Lucretian Epicureanism** _(also: Roman Epicureanism)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Epicureanism ▸ Lucretian Epicureanism
  Roman poetic articulation of Epicurean atomism arguing that understanding a purely material, godless nature dispels superstition and fear of death.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Lucretius, Philodemus · 1st c. BCE · Roman Republic</sub>
- **Megarian School** _(also: Megarians)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Megarian School
  Socratic school fusing Eleatic monism with dialectic, identifying the good with a single unchanging being and pioneering logical paradoxes.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Euclid of Megara, Eubulides, Stilpo · 4th c. BCE · Megara, Greece</sub>
- **Middle Platonism** _(also: Middle Platonic Metaphysics)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Middle Platonism
  Revival of Platonism positing transcendent Forms and an impersonal first principle as the intelligible source and structure of reality.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Antiochus of Ascalon, Plutarch, Alcinous, Numenius · 1st c. BCE–2nd c. CE · Greco-Roman world</sub>
- **Modern Stoicism** _(also: Stoicism 2.0, Contemporary Stoicism)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Stoicism ▸ Modern Stoicism
  Contemporary revival adapting Stoic practices to secular self-improvement and cognitive therapy, dropping the ancient cosmology's providential physics.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Lawrence Becker, Massimo Pigliucci, William Irvine · 20th–21st c. CE · Western world</sub>
- **Panaetian Middle Stoicism** _(also: Middle Stoa)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Stoicism ▸ Middle Stoicism
  Transitional Stoicism softening early rigor with Platonic and Peripatetic elements and emphasizing practical duties suited to social roles.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Panaetius of Rhodes, Posidonius · 2nd–1st c. BCE · Rhodes, Rome</sub>
- **Peripateticism** _(also: Aristotelian Ethics, The Lyceum)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Peripateticism
  Aristotelian tradition holding that flourishing (eudaimonia) comes from cultivating virtue as a rational mean, guided by empirical study of nature.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Aristotle, Theophrastus, Strato of Lampsacus · 4th c. BCE · Athens</sub>
- **Pyrrhonism** _(also: Pyrrhonian Skepticism)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Skepticism ▸ Pyrrhonism
  Radical skeptical practice suspending judgment on all non-evident matters to reach tranquility, treating appearances as the sole guide to action.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Pyrrho of Elis, Timon, Aenesidemus, Sextus Empiricus · 4th c. BCE · Ancient Greece</sub>
- **Roman Stoicism** _(also: Late Stoa, Imperial Stoicism)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Stoicism ▸ Roman Stoicism
  Ethically focused Roman Stoicism emphasizing practical self-discipline, duty, and inner tranquility over the earlier school's logic and physics.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Seneca, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius, Musonius Rufus · 1st–2nd c. CE · Roman Empire</sub>
- **Stratonism** _(also: Straton's Naturalism)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Peripateticism ▸ Stratonism
  Naturalistic wing of the Lyceum explaining the cosmos wholly through immanent natural forces and heat, without an active divine mover.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Strato of Lampsacus · 3rd c. BCE · Athens</sub>

## Indian

_16 schools_

- **Abhidharma** _(also: Abhidhamma, Buddhist phenomenology)_ — Indian ▸ Nastika ▸ Buddhism ▸ Abhidharma
  The Buddhist analytic project reducing experience to momentary impersonal factors (dharmas), denying an enduring self while cataloguing mind and matter.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Vasubandhu (Kosa), Buddhaghosa, Sarvastivada masters · c. 3rd c. BCE - 5th c. CE · Ancient India</sub>
- **Advaita Vedanta** _(also: Non-dualism, Kevaladvaita)_ — Indian ▸ Astika ▸ Vedanta ▸ Advaita
  A non-dual monism holding that the individual self is identical with the impersonal absolute (Brahman), with the plural world superimposed by ignorance.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Gaudapada, Shankara, Mandana Mishra · c. 8th c. CE (roots earlier) · Ancient/Medieval India</sub>
- **Ajivika** _(also: Ajivikism)_ — Indian ▸ Nastika ▸ Ajivika
  A fatalist school teaching that all events are fixed by an impersonal cosmic principle (niyati), so effort cannot alter one's destined liberation.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Makkhali Gosala, Purana Kassapa, Pakudha Kaccayana · 5th c. BCE · Ancient India (Magadha)</sub>
- **Anekantavada** _(also: Jain non-absolutism, many-sidedness)_ — Indian ▸ Nastika ▸ Jainism ▸ Anekantavada
  The Jain doctrine that reality is many-sided, so any claim captures only a partial standpoint and no single view is absolutely complete.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Mahavira, Siddhasena Divakara, Haribhadra · c. 5th c. BCE onward · Ancient India</sub>
- **Buddhist Pramana (Dignaga-Dharmakirti)** _(also: Buddhist logico-epistemology, Sautrantika-Yogacara logic)_ — Indian ▸ Nastika ▸ Buddhism ▸ Pramana-Logic
  A Buddhist epistemology admitting only perception and inference, grounded in momentary particulars, with concepts treated as useful exclusions (apoha).

- **Carvaka** _(also: Lokayata, Barhaspatya)_ — Indian ▸ Nastika ▸ Carvaka-Lokayata
  A materialist school holding that only perceptible matter is real, rejecting soul, afterlife, karma, and inference as reliable knowledge.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Brhaspati (legendary), Ajita Kesakambali, Jayarasi · 6th c. BCE onward · Ancient India</sub>
- **Classical Yoga (Patanjala)** _(also: Raja Yoga, Yoga darshana)_ — Indian ▸ Astika ▸ Samkhya ▸ Patanjala-Yoga
  A Samkhya-based praxis system aiming at cessation of mental fluctuations to isolate pure consciousness, treating any 'lord' as a meditative aid, not creator.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Patanjali, Vyasa · c. 2nd c. BCE - 4th c. CE · Ancient India</sub>
- **Early Buddhism** _(also: Pre-sectarian Buddhism, Nikaya teaching)_ — Indian ▸ Nastika ▸ Buddhism ▸ Early-Buddhism
  The foundational teaching of the Four Noble Truths, dependent origination, impermanence, and not-self, aiming at the cessation of suffering.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha) · 5th c. BCE · Ancient India</sub>
- **Jain Metaphysics** _(also: Jaina tattva, jiva-ajiva doctrine)_ — Indian ▸ Nastika ▸ Jainism ▸ Metaphysics
  A pluralist realism of countless eternal souls and material karma, where liberation comes from ascetic purification rather than any creator deity.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Mahavira, Umasvati, Kundakunda · 6th c. BCE onward · Ancient India</sub>
- **Madhyamaka** _(also: Middle Way school, Sunyavada)_ — Indian ▸ Nastika ▸ Buddhism ▸ Madhyamaka
  The Mahayana philosophy that all phenomena are empty of intrinsic nature, arising dependently, avoiding both eternalism and nihilism.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Nagarjuna, Aryadeva, Candrakirti, Bhaviveka · c. 2nd c. CE onward · Ancient India</sub>
- **Nyaya** _(also: Nyaya darshana)_ — Indian ▸ Astika ▸ Nyaya
  A realist school of logic and epistemology systematizing valid means of knowledge and inference as the path to liberation from suffering.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Gautama Aksapada, Vatsyayana, Udayana · c. 2nd c. CE onward · Ancient India</sub>
- **Purva Mimamsa** _(also: Mimamsa, Karma-Mimamsa)_ — Indian ▸ Astika ▸ Mimamsa ▸ Purva-Mimamsa
  A ritual-exegetical school treating dharma as duty enjoined by eternal, authorless Vedic injunction, needing no creator god to ground obligation.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Jaimini, Shabara, Kumarila Bhatta, Prabhakara · c. 3rd c. BCE onward · Ancient India</sub>
- **Samkhya** _(also: Sankhya)_ — Indian ▸ Astika ▸ Samkhya
  An atheistic dualism distinguishing plural conscious selves (purusha) from unconscious primordial nature (prakriti), with liberation through discriminative knowledge.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Kapila, Ishvarakrishna · c. 4th c. BCE onward · Ancient India</sub>
- **Syadvada** _(also: Sevenfold predication, saptabhangi)_ — Indian ▸ Nastika ▸ Jainism ▸ Syadvada
  The Jain logical method qualifying every assertion with 'in some respect' (syat), yielding seven conditional predications to express many-sided truth.

- **Vaisheshika** _(also: Vaisesika, atomism)_ — Indian ▸ Astika ▸ Vaisheshika
  A pluralist naturalism analyzing reality into categories and eternal atoms combining by unseen force, later paired with Nyaya as a logic-atomism system.

- **Yogacara** _(also: Vijnanavada, Cittamatra, consciousness-only)_ — Indian ▸ Nastika ▸ Buddhism ▸ Yogacara
  The Mahayana idealism holding that experienced objects are constructions of consciousness, with liberation via transforming the mind's storehouse tendencies.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Asanga, Vasubandhu, Sthiramati · c. 4th c. CE onward · Ancient India</sub>

## Ancient Greek

_15 schools_

- **Anaxagorean Nous-Cosmology** _(also: Doctrine of Seeds, Mind-ordered Pluralism)_ — Presocratic ▸ Pluralist School ▸ Anaxagoras
  Anaxagoras held that all things contain portions of everything and that a cosmic Mind (Nous) initiated and orders the rotation separating the primordial mixture.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Anaxagoras of Clazomenae · 5th c. BCE · Clazomenae, Ionia / Athens</sub>
- **Anaximander's Apeiron** _(also: The Boundless)_ — Presocratic ▸ Milesian School ▸ Anaximander
  Anaximander posited the apeiron, an indefinite boundless principle, as the source of all things, with opposites separating out and returning in cosmic justice.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Anaximander of Miletus · 6th c. BCE · Miletus, Ionia</sub>
- **Anaximenes' Air-Monism** _(also: Aer Principle)_ — Presocratic ▸ Milesian School ▸ Anaximenes
  Anaximenes identified air as the fundamental substance, with rarefaction and condensation explaining how it becomes all other things.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Anaximenes of Miletus · 6th c. BCE · Miletus, Ionia</sub>
- **Ancient Atomism** _(also: Democritean Atomism, Leucippus-Democritus theory)_ — Presocratic ▸ Atomist School ▸ Leucippus and Democritus
  The atomists held that reality consists of indivisible atoms moving in empty void, with all qualities and events arising from their mechanical arrangement and collision.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Leucippus, Democritus · 5th c. BCE · Abdera, Thrace</sub>
- **Aristotelian Hylomorphism** _(also: Peripatetic metaphysics, Matter-Form doctrine)_ — Classical ▸ Aristotelianism ▸ Hylomorphism
  Aristotle analyzed substances as compounds of matter and form, explaining change through four causes and potentiality actualized, grounding knowledge in the study of nature.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Aristotle, Theophrastus · 4th c. BCE · Athens / Macedon</sub>
- **Eleatic Monism** _(also: Parmenidean Being, Way of Truth)_ — Presocratic ▸ Eleatic School ▸ Parmenides
  Parmenides argued that Being is one, changeless, and indivisible, and that becoming and plurality are illusions of the senses contradicted by reason.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Parmenides of Elea · 5th c. BCE · Elea, Magna Graecia</sub>
- **Empedoclean Pluralism** _(also: Four Roots Cosmology, Love and Strife)_ — Presocratic ▸ Pluralist School ▸ Empedocles
  Empedocles proposed four eternal elements (earth, air, fire, water) mixed and separated by the cosmic forces of Love and Strife, explaining change without absolute becoming.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Empedocles of Acragas · 5th c. BCE · Acragas, Sicily</sub>
- **Gorgianic Nihilism** _(also: Gorgias' Skeptical Sophism, On Non-Being)_ — Classical ▸ Sophism ▸ Gorgias
  Gorgias argued that nothing exists, that if anything existed it could not be known, and that if known it could not be communicated, foregrounding rhetoric over truth.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Gorgias of Leontini · 5th c. BCE · Leontini, Sicily / Athens</sub>
- **Heraclitean Flux** _(also: Doctrine of Becoming, Logos philosophy)_ — Presocratic ▸ Ephesian School ▸ Heraclitus
  Heraclitus taught that reality is ceaseless change governed by a rational Logos, with fire as the primary element and unity emerging from the strife of opposites.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Heraclitus of Ephesus · c. 500 BCE · Ephesus, Ionia</sub>
- **Platonic Theory of Forms** _(also: Platonism, Theory of Ideas)_ — Classical ▸ Platonism ▸ Theory of Forms
  Plato held that the sensible world imitates eternal, non-material Forms, with true knowledge attained by reason grasping these unchanging archetypes, chief of which is the Good.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Plato · 4th c. BCE · Athens</sub>
- **Protagorean Relativism** _(also: Homo-mensura doctrine, Sophistic relativism)_ — Classical ▸ Sophism ▸ Protagoras
  Protagoras taught that man is the measure of all things, making truth relative to individual perception and treating knowledge and morality as human conventions.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Protagoras of Abdera · 5th c. BCE · Abdera / Athens</sub>
- **Pythagoreanism** _(also: Pythagorean number-mysticism, Harmony of the Cosmos)_ — Presocratic ▸ Italic School ▸ Pythagoreanism
  The Pythagoreans held that number and mathematical harmony are the underlying structure of reality, uniting cosmology, music, and a disciplined way of life.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Pythagoras, Philolaus, Archytas · 6th–5th c. BCE · Croton, Magna Graecia</sub>
- **Thalesian Water-Monism** _(also: Milesian Water Principle)_ — Presocratic ▸ Milesian School ▸ Thales
  Thales held that water is the single underlying arche from which all things arise, seeking a natural rather than mythological first principle.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Thales of Miletus · 6th c. BCE · Miletus, Ionia</sub>
- **Xenophanic Nature-Inquiry** _(also: Xenophanes' critique of anthropomorphism)_ — Presocratic ▸ Eleatic School ▸ Xenophanes
  Xenophanes criticized anthropomorphic mythology and pursued naturalistic explanations of phenomena, while stressing the limits and fallibility of human knowledge.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Xenophanes of Colophon · 6th–5th c. BCE · Colophon, Ionia / Elea</sub>
- **Zenonian Paradox Dialectic** _(also: Zeno's Paradoxes, Eleatic Defense)_ — Presocratic ▸ Eleatic School ▸ Zeno of Elea
  Zeno defended Eleatic monism through paradoxes of motion and plurality, arguing that assuming multiplicity and change leads to logical contradiction.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Zeno of Elea, Melissus of Samos · 5th c. BCE · Elea, Magna Graecia</sub>

_14 schools_

  Studies the structures of consciousness and intentionality through the epoché, bracketing the natural attitude to describe how phenomena appear to experience.

- **Will to Power** _(also: Nietzschean Vitalism)_ — Nietzschean Philosophy ▸ Will to Power
  Interprets life and reality as driven by a fundamental striving to grow, overcome, and expand power, culminating in self-overcoming and value-creation.

## Mind & Free Will

_14 schools_

- **Anomalous Monism** — Philosophy of Mind ▸ Physicalism ▸ Anomalous Monism
  Mental events are token-identical to physical events, yet there are no strict psychophysical laws, leaving the mental causally efficacious but nomologically irreducible.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Donald Davidson · 1970s · US</sub>
- **Behaviorism** _(also: Logical behaviorism, philosophical behaviorism)_ — Philosophy of Mind ▸ Behaviorism
  Mental terms are analyzed as dispositions to observable behavior, denying or bracketing private inner states as explanatorily needed.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Gilbert Ryle, B.F. Skinner, Carl Hempel · early-mid 20th c. · UK, US</sub>
- **Compatibilism** _(also: Soft determinism, reconciliationism)_ — Free Will ▸ Compatibilism
  Free will and determinism are compatible because freedom consists in acting from one's own reasons and desires without external coercion.
  <sub>Key thinkers: David Hume, Harry Frankfurt, Daniel Dennett · 18th c.-present · Anglophone</sub>
- **Eliminative Materialism** _(also: Eliminativism)_ — Philosophy of Mind ▸ Physicalism ▸ Eliminative Materialism
  Folk-psychological states like beliefs and desires do not really exist and will be replaced by a mature neuroscience.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Paul Churchland, Patricia Churchland, Richard Rorty · late 20th c. · North America</sub>
- **Emergentism** _(also: British emergentism, strong emergence)_ — Philosophy of Mind ▸ Emergentism
  Mental properties genuinely emerge from complex physical organization while being novel and irreducible to their underlying components.
  <sub>Key thinkers: C.D. Broad, Samuel Alexander, C. Lloyd Morgan · 1920s · Britain</sub>
- **Functionalism** _(also: Machine-state functionalism)_ — Philosophy of Mind ▸ Physicalism ▸ Functionalism
  Mental states are defined by their causal-functional roles rather than by their physical composition, allowing multiple realizability across substrates.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Hilary Putnam, Jerry Fodor, David Lewis · 1960s-present · US</sub>
- **Hard Determinism** _(also: Incompatibilist determinism)_ — Free Will ▸ Incompatibilism ▸ Hard Determinism
  Every event is causally necessitated, so genuine free will is impossible and moral responsibility in the desert sense is unwarranted.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Baron d'Holbach, Derk Pereboom, Galen Strawson · 18th c.-present · Europe, US</sub>
- **Illusionism about Consciousness** _(also: Strong illusionism, eliminativism about qualia)_ — Philosophy of Mind ▸ Physicalism ▸ Illusionism
  Phenomenal consciousness as usually conceived is an introspective illusion, and the real task is explaining why it seems so vivid.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Keith Frankish, Daniel Dennett · 21st c. · Anglophone</sub>
- **Libertarian Free Will** _(also: Metaphysical libertarianism, agent-causal libertarianism)_ — Free Will ▸ Incompatibilism ▸ Libertarianism
  Determinism is false and agents possess genuinely undetermined choices, sourcing actions in the agent rather than prior causes alone.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Robert Kane, Roderick Chisholm, Timothy O'Connor · 20th c.-present · US</sub>
- **Neutral Monism** — Philosophy of Mind ▸ Neutral Monism
  Mind and matter are both constructions from a single underlying kind of stuff that is itself neither mental nor physical.
  <sub>Key thinkers: William James, Bertrand Russell, Ernst Mach · late 19th-20th c. · US, UK, Austria</sub>
- **Panpsychism** _(also: Panexperientialism)_ — Philosophy of Mind ▸ Panpsychism
  Consciousness or experience is a fundamental and ubiquitous feature of reality, present in some form even in basic physical constituents.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Galen Strawson, Philip Goff, Alfred North Whitehead · ancient-present · Global</sub>
- **Physicalism** _(also: Materialism about mind)_ — Philosophy of Mind ▸ Physicalism
  The view that everything, including mental states, is ultimately physical or wholly determined by and grounded in physical facts.
  <sub>Key thinkers: J.J.C. Smart, David Armstrong, David Lewis · 20th c. · Anglophone</sub>
- **Property Dualism** _(also: Non-reductive physicalism of properties)_ — Philosophy of Mind ▸ Dualism ▸ Property Dualism
  There is one physical substance, but it bears irreducible mental properties, so consciousness is a distinct non-physical feature of physical things.
  <sub>Key thinkers: David Chalmers, Frank Jackson, Thomas Nagel · late 20th c. · Anglophone</sub>
- **Type Identity Theory** _(also: Mind-brain identity theory, type physicalism)_ — Philosophy of Mind ▸ Physicalism ▸ Type Identity Theory
  Mental state types are strictly identical to physical brain state types, so each kind of experience just is a kind of neural process.
  <sub>Key thinkers: U.T. Place, J.J.C. Smart, Herbert Feigl · mid-20th c. · Australia, US</sub>

## Skeptical & Therapeutic

_13 schools_

- **Agnosticism** _(also: Epistemic Agnosticism)_ — Modern ▸ Philosophy of Religion ▸ Agnosticism
  The position that the existence of the divine or ultimate reality is unknown and possibly unknowable, withholding assent either way.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Thomas Huxley, Bertrand Russell · 19th c. · Britain</sub>
- **Amor Fati** _(also: Love of Fate)_ — Modern ▸ Existentialism ▸ Amor Fati
  The affirmative embrace of everything that happens, including suffering, as something to be willed and loved rather than merely endured.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Friedrich Nietzsche (Stoic roots) · 19th c. · Germany</sub>
- **Apatheism** _(also: Pragmatic Atheism, Apathetic Agnosticism)_ — Modern ▸ Philosophy of Religion ▸ Apatheism
  A stance of practical indifference to whether any god exists, treating the question as irrelevant to how one should live.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Jonathan Rauch (popularizer) · 20th–21st c. · Western</sub>
- **Cyrenaic Presentism** _(also: Cyrenaic Skepticism)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Skepticism ▸ Cyrenaic Presentism
  A skeptical hedonism holding that only one's present subjective sensations are knowable, so wisdom focuses on the pleasure of the moment.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Aristippus, Hegesias, Anniceris · 4th c. BCE · Ancient Greece / Cyrene</sub>
- **Fallibilism** _(also: Epistemic Fallibilism)_ — Modern ▸ Epistemology ▸ Fallibilism
  The view that no belief is immune to revision, so all knowledge claims remain provisional and open to future correction.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Charles Peirce, Karl Popper · 19th–20th c. · United States / Europe</sub>
- **Ignosticism** _(also: Igtheism, Theological Noncognitivism)_ — Modern ▸ Philosophy of Religion ▸ Ignosticism
  The view that the question of God's existence is meaningless until the term 'God' is given a coherent, testable definition.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Sherwin Wine, A. J. Ayer (related) · 20th c. · United States</sub>
- **Montaignean Skepticism** _(also: Que sais-je? Skepticism)_ — Renaissance ▸ Skepticism ▸ Montaignean Skepticism
  A humane, essayistic skepticism doubting the reach of human reason and counseling modest self-examination and tolerant living.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Michel de Montaigne · 16th c. · France</sub>
- **Philosophical Counseling** _(also: Philosophical Practice, Socratic Counseling)_ — Contemporary ▸ Applied Philosophy ▸ Philosophical Counseling
  A contemporary practice applying philosophical dialogue and reasoning to help individuals examine beliefs and address life problems.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Gerd Achenbach, Lou Marinoff · 20th–21st c. · Germany / North America</sub>
- **Stoic Negative Visualization** _(also: Premeditatio Malorum)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Stoicism ▸ Stoic Negative Visualization
  The Stoic practice of deliberately imagining loss and misfortune to reduce attachment, increase gratitude, and prepare the mind for adversity.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Seneca, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius · 1st–2nd c. CE · Ancient Rome</sub>
- **Therapy of Desire** _(also: Hellenistic Therapeutic Ethics)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Therapeutic Ethics ▸ Therapy of Desire
  The Hellenistic project treating philosophy as medicine for the soul that cures irrational desires and false beliefs causing suffering.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Epicurus, Chrysippus, Martha Nussbaum · 3rd c. BCE · Ancient Greece / Rome</sub>
- **Wittgensteinian Quietism** _(also: Therapeutic Wittgensteinianism)_ — Modern ▸ Analytic Philosophy ▸ Wittgensteinian Quietism
  The view that philosophy poses no theses but dissolves confusions by clarifying ordinary language, leaving everything as it is.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Ludwig Wittgenstein, John McDowell · 20th c. · Austria / Britain</sub>
- **Wu-wei Practice** _(also: Effortless Action, Non-doing)_ — East Asian ▸ Daoism ▸ Wu-wei Practice
  The Daoist cultivation of effortless, spontaneous action aligned with the natural flow of the Tao rather than forced striving.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Laozi, Zhuangzi · 4th c. BCE · Ancient China</sub>
- **Zeteticism** _(also: Zetetic Skepticism, Inquiry Skepticism)_ — Hellenistic ▸ Skepticism ▸ Zeteticism
  The skeptic's posture of continued open inquiry that neither affirms nor denies but keeps investigating without dogmatic conclusion.
  <sub>Key thinkers: Sextus Empiricus · 2nd c. CE · Ancient Greece</sub>
